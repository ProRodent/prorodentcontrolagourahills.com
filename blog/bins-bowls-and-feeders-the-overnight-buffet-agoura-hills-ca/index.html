Bins, Bowls and Feeders: The Overnight Buffet

Rodents rarely move in because of one big attraction. They follow a trail of small meals, and most of those meals come from household habits that take seconds to change.

Written by Agoura Hills Pro Rodent Control on September 25, 2026. Allow about 4 minutes..

Think of your property as a restaurant that never closes. The menu is unintentional: a trash can with a loose lid, a dog bowl left on the patio, a bird feeder that empties by sunrise, a compost pile open to the sky. Each item is a small meal, and together they add up to a reason for rodents to stay.

The five biggest sources.

Source.Why it attracts.Habit that helps.
Trash and recycling bins.Food residue, open lids, overflow bags.Tight lids, rinse cans, no bags on the ground.
Pet bowls and kibble.Easy calories, left out overnight.Feed on a schedule, store the bag in a bin.
Bird feeders.Seed spills under the feeder.Use trays, clean the ground, bring in at dusk.
Compost.Scraps and warmth.Closed bin, no meat or dairy.
Fruit and nut trees.Windfall on the ground.Pick up every few days.

Start with the one that matches your yard. If you have a lemon tree and a bird feeder, those are your first two jobs.

Bins: the lid is the whole point.

A trash can with a lid that does not close is a buffet with the doors open. Check each lid for cracks and a tight fit. If the can overflows between pickups, add a second bin or reduce what goes in. Bagged trash piled beside the can is easy pickings for rodents, raccoons and other animals.

  • Rinse cans every month or two.
  • Keep the bins on a hard surface, away from the wall.
  • Put food waste in sealed bags before it goes in.
  • Wipe up spills on the pavement at once.

Recycling bins matter too. Unrinsed cans and jars carry residue, and a few drops of juice are enough to interest a mouse.

Pet food: schedules beat all-day bowls.

A bowl that sits out all day and night draws animals, whether you have a cat, a dog or chickens. Feed at set times, pick up the bowl, and sweep up crumbs. Store the bag in a lidded metal or hard plastic bin, not in its paper sack.

If you feed outdoor cats, set the bowl out only for the time they eat. Overnight bowls are the single most common sign we hear about from households with pets.

Feeders and spills.

Bird seed is rich in fat and protein, and it falls. A tray under the feeder catches most of it, and a quick sweep every evening catches the rest. Hanging the feeder away from walls and fences also reduces access.

If you notice seed disappearing at night, droppings under the feeder or chewed plastic, a rodent is probably eating there. Bring the feeder in for a couple of weeks and see whether signs fade.

Compost and fruit.

Composting is a good habit, and a closed bin is the way to do it. Choose a model with a secure lid and a floor or mesh that keeps animals from tunneling in. Do not add meat, bones, dairy or cooked leftovers.

Fruit that falls and stays on the ground draws rodents quickly. Pick it up every few days, and harvest ripe fruit promptly. The rat page explains how outdoor findings feed into a plan.

What this will not do.

Removing food does not remove animals already inside, and it does not close gaps. It lowers the odds that new animals are drawn to your property. For animals already in the house, the next step is an inspection, and the inspection page explains what happens.

If you cut the buffet and still hear scratching, call. Tell the technician what you changed, because that is useful information about how the animals are surviving.

A ten-minute evening routine.

  • Close and latch every bin.
  • Pick up pet bowls and wipe the floor.
  • Sweep seed from under feeders.
  • Walk the yard for windfall fruit.
  • Check that the compost lid is on.

Do it at dusk, before the animals start their night. After two weeks it becomes automatic, and it removes most of the easy meals.

Five safeguards for droppings and nest scraps.

Many homeowners handle small cleanups themselves, even when a technician is on the way, so keep these precautions nearby.

  • Pellets and nests turn to powder when dry, so no broom and no shop vac.
  • Public health pages put airing out first on the list, with roughly thirty minutes as the benchmark.
  • Do not touch nesting material with bare fingers, even if it looks dry.
  • Old bleach mix loses strength, so make a new batch for each session.
  • Two bags keep the waste contained until pickup.
  • Washing is the final step, whether or not you wore gloves.

Still unsure about something?

Do I need to stop feeding birds?

Not necessarily. Use a tray to catch spills, pick feeders that limit scatter and bring them in at dusk. Keep them well away from the walls.

Is a compost bin a problem?

An open pile can be. A closed bin with a secure lid and a hard floor or mesh base is safer, and avoid adding meat, dairy or cooked food.

How far should the trash cans sit from the house?

A few feet from the wall helps, and a tight lid matters more than distance. Rinse cans occasionally to cut odors.

Should I stop leaving pet food out?

Feed pets at set times and pick up the bowl afterward. Store the bag in a lidded metal or hard plastic bin.

Does a clean yard guarantee no rodents?

No. It reduces attraction, but gaps in the house are what allow entry. Both matter.
