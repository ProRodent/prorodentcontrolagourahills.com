What we know about Old Agoura, and what we do not.

Your own property is what counts, and you describe it to us in a few minutes. The address you give decides where the technician drives and which coverage entry applies.

Reasoning from the parcel, not the name.

Parcels in foothill communities vary a great deal. Some have long driveways, mature oaks and detached garages. Others have small yards, shared walls or a two-car garage close to the street. We cannot say which kind you have, so the call includes a few questions about lot size and outbuildings.

  • Is there a detached garage, workshop, barn or shed.
  • How long is the fence line, and what grows along it.
  • Are there animal enclosures, feed bins or a compost area.
  • Do trees overhang the roof.

Each answer changes where the technician starts. A long fence line with ivy suggests an outdoor-first plan, while a compact lot with an attached garage points inside.

Outbuildings and feed storage.

Sheds, workshops and similar outbuildings that hold seed, feed or pet food can offer more to eat than the house itself. We do not claim any property here keeps animals. If yours does, store feed in sturdy lidded bins of metal or thick plastic and sweep up what spills.

Seasons and the move indoors.

In Southern California, hot dry stretches and sudden storms both change where animals find comfort. No claim is made that calls rise on any street in any month. It remains simpler to shut gaps before an animal settles in than after.

  • Circle the lot each spring and fall, eyes on the foot of every wall.
  • Decant pet food and birdseed into tubs with lids.
  • Stack firewood on a rack well off the house.
  • Look for daylight along the garage door seal.
