Landscaping is where the plan starts.

Residential streets in the region commonly mix lawns, hedges, mature trees and planted strips along walls. We cannot confirm that for Fountainwood, so treat this as general reasoning. What matters is how plant cover meets the building, because cover gives rodents a sheltered approach.

  • Hedges within a few inches of the wall.
  • Ivy or vines climbing stucco or siding.
  • Branches that touch the roof or gutters.
  • Planters and pots stacked against the foundation.
  • Thick mulch pushed up against the base of the wall.

A clear strip along the wall makes inspection easier and removes cover. The technician marks where the cover is thickest.

Sprinklers, drip lines and standing water.

Irrigation keeps plants healthy and can also create damp spots that attract animals. Leaky valve boxes, drip lines that pool and overflowing planters supply water close to the wall. Fixing a leak costs little and removes one reason to visit.

What to do in the meantime.

Stay clear of droppings and keep little ones and animals out of the room. Poison bait is best left in the store, since a poisoned animal might die in a wall, and sticky boards snare other wildlife. Cleaning that cannot wait should follow the CDC box below.

Have the room, the time and the first thing you noticed ready for the call. Keep it brief.

The visit in four moves.

The newest evidence is the starting line. From there the technician walks the building, records openings and sets traps on genuine runs. Sealing is held back until early catches show which entries are live, then a second visit checks it. This approach applies across Agoura Hills and bends to plant cover and to shared or detached construction.

Until the crew arrives.

  • Mute the television for a few minutes and listen at the wall that sounds worst.
  • Never run a vacuum across droppings, however few.
  • Look at pantry boxes for gnaw marks and move open food into jars or hard bins.
  • Count the places where fresh signs appear and tell us the tally.

Federal cleanup advice, condensed.

A little preparation lowers the risk from rodent waste. Order matters: ventilate, wet, wipe, bag, wash.

  • A single dry pellet can crumble at a touch and release a cloud you cannot see.
  • Plan on half an hour of airing before any wiping begins.
  • Gloves go on first, come off last and end up in the trash bag.
  • Wet every inch you plan to touch, including the spot underneath the pellets.
  • Seal the bag before washing your hands.
  • Soap and running water beat sanitizer gel here.
