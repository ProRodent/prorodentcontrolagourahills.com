Why the smell does not leave on its own.

A rodent that dies in a wall cavity, attic corner or crawl space does not dry the way one outdoors might. Still air, insulation and building materials soak up the odor and let it out gradually. Warm weather speeds things along, and a smell may appear in a day or two.

The source is usually one spot. That is the hopeful part. The challenge is finding it, because sound travels through a house in surprising ways and so does odor.

Narrowing the hunt.

  • Tour the rooms at the same hour each day and mark where the odor peaks.
  • Check closets, vents and the space around the water heater.
  • Notice whether the smell rises with the furnace or air conditioner.
  • Look for flies near windows, a ceiling light or a vent.
  • Smell near baseboards, outlet covers and the access panel of a bathtub.

The technician uses the same clues and adds tools: a flashlight, a thermal sense of airflow and a trained nose.

What not to do.

Do not spray strong chemicals into a wall void, and do not handle the animal bare-handed. A carcass can leak fluids and carry germs. Open the windows, run a fan and send the little ones and the pets to another room.

If you suspect poison caused the death, tell us. Bait packets near the area help the technician understand what happened and keep other animals safe.

After the carcass is gone.

A faint odor can linger in insulation or wood for days. The treatment step reduces it. If an area was heavily soiled, the cleanup page explains how affected material is replaced.

Stay alert for a second carcass. Rodents often travel in groups, and a single removal does not always mean the problem is over. The return visit checks.

Until the crew arrives.

  • Snap the lids on the trash bins tonight and roll them away from the garage wall.
  • If you do clean, wet the spot with disinfectant first and use paper towels.
  • Raise pet dishes to a shelf for the night.
  • Look at the bottom edge of the garage door and describe any daylight you can see.

Federal cleanup advice, condensed.

Many homeowners handle small cleanups themselves, even when a technician is on the way, so keep these precautions nearby.

  • Feather dusters flick old droppings into the air, so keep them away too.
  • Make a draft with two openings, then busy yourself elsewhere until the time is up.
  • Gloves go on first, come off last and end up in the trash bag.
  • Paper towels plus disinfectant spray, and the towels go into a bag at once.
  • Gloves join the other waste after the surface is wiped.
  • Rinse the sprayer and any tools, then wash yourself.

Odor queries from callers.

How long does the smell last?

Duration varies with the animal's size, the heat and how air moves. A mouse may fade within a week or two, while a rat sealed in a cavity can last longer. Only removal fixes it for sure.

Can I mask the smell with air fresheners?

They may mask it briefly, but the source is still there. Locating and removing it is the real solution.

Why are there small flies?

Flies can breed in a carcass. Seeing them clustered near a wall or ceiling suggests the source is close.

Will you have to open the wall?

Sometimes a small access hole is needed to reach the carcass. The estimate states this in advance, and the repair is listed separately.

Is the smell dangerous?

It is unpleasant, but the bigger concern is germs from decomposition and droppings. Follow the CDC steps if you clean.

Calls about odors.

Which room smells worst? Do you spot tiny flies? Does the HVAC seem to move the smell around? Every answer shrinks the search area.
