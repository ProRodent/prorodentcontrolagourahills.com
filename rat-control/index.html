Two kinds of rat, two ways of arriving.

The two rat species announce themselves differently. A roof rat is a climber, so its route often runs along a fence top, a utility line or a tree limb straight to the eave. A Norway rat is a digger that prefers the ground, and its trail often begins at a burrow beside a slab or under a deck. Southern California homeowners may meet either, and we confirm which one on site.

The distinction matters because it decides where the first hour goes. For a climber, the roofline, the attic vents and the tree canopy come first. For a digger, the foundation, the deck skirting and the shed come first.

The outdoor lap.

The technician starts with an unhurried lap of the lot. The technician looks up as much as down: limbs within reach of the shingles, ivy or vines climbing the wall, palm fronds tucked against an eave, and cables that make a convenient rope.

  • Fence tops and block-wall caps, which rats use as quiet highways.
  • Vines, ivy and dense ground cover against the wall.
  • Fruit trees or feeders within a short jump of the roof.
  • Burrow openings with smooth, packed edges beside slabs, sheds and planters.

Findings go on a sketch with numbers matched to the estimate, so you can see where each recommendation comes from.

Inside: where the evidence tends to hide.

Rats leave larger pellets than mice, roughly the size of an olive pit, along with smears of oil where fur brushes the same beam day after day. Insulation may show tunnels or compressed paths. In the attic the technician follows the oil trail to the opening and notes any wiring that has been gnawed.

Chewed wiring is a safety matter, not just a nuisance. If you see bare copper, stay away from it and tell us on the call, so the visit can be planned with a licensed electrician in mind when needed.

Sealing a roofline for good.

Rats can enlarge a gap by gnawing, which is why foam or caulk alone does not hold. Eave returns, roof-to-wall seams and vent openings get metal: heavy mesh, flashing or a formed screen fastened with screws. Ground-level gaps get the same treatment. The exclusion page describes how each material is fitted, and the trapping page explains where devices go.

Yard habits that shorten the list.

Pick up windfall fruit, keep compost closed and store pet food indoors overnight. Move wood stacks and storage boxes off the wall. If a tree touches the roof, ask a qualified tree service to cut it back, since working at roof height is not a homeowner project. Each step removes an incentive, though a closed gap is what ends the problem.

A property near open parkland or a hillside edge may see more pressure from wildlife and rodents alike, but we have no survey saying so for any street. The practical response is the same: shorten every route from the cover to your walls.

Next steps for tonight.

  • Snap the lids on the trash bins tonight and roll them away from the garage wall.

Read this before you clean.

Dried rodent waste breaks into fine particles, so the safe way to deal with it is slow and damp. These steps follow public health guidance and apply even if you never book a visit.

  • Pellets and nests turn to powder when dry, so no broom and no shop vac.
  • Public health pages put airing out first on the list, with roughly thirty minutes as the benchmark.
  • Choose gloves long enough to cover the wrist, where dust likes to land.
  • Wet every inch you plan to touch, including the spot underneath the pellets.
  • Knot the bag firmly and put it in an outdoor bin with a lid.
  • Rinse the sprayer and any tools, then wash yourself.

Rat questions from Agoura Hills households.

Roof rat or Norway rat?

A roof rat is slim with a long tail and climbs well, so it favors trees, wires and rooflines. A Norway rat is heavier, shorter-tailed and a digger that stays low. Pellets, burrows and the height of the noise tell the technician which is present.

Does fruit on the ground matter?

It can. Windfall fruit, unpicked citrus and fallen nuts provide a meal close to cover. Picking up fallen fruit often and harvesting ripe fruit promptly removes one attraction, though it will not close a gap.

Is cleaning up after rats in the attic risky?

The danger is dust lifting off dry pellets. This page carries the CDC steps in a box, and a large mess is a job for a technician.

Will trimming shrubs and tree limbs help?

Limbs that brush the roof are a bridge, and shrubs against the wall shelter the last few feet of a trip. Trimming shortens the route yet leaves any soffit or foundation gap untouched, so both jobs count.

Can I get a visit tonight?

Someone picks up at every hour, and with space on the schedule a visit can leave the same day.

What to tell us about a rat problem.

Mention citrus trees, ivy, wood stacks, pet bowls and any branch that touches the roof. Those details tell the technician which side of the house to read first.
