What a mouse is shopping for.

A house mouse looks for shelter that stays dry, a meal close by and a concealed path linking them. The void behind a lower kitchen cabinet satisfies all three. So does a garage corner where seasonal bins lean on the drywall, with cardboard to chew into bedding.

Weather around here adds a wrinkle. Dry stretches, sprinklers that keep one patch of soil damp and the occasional soaking rain each shift where a mouse feels comfortable. We cannot say which month your problem will start, and a house left empty during a long trip may host a colony before anyone notices, since mice come out after the household sleeps.

Try sitting quietly for a few minutes by the pantry, then the utility closet, then the garage wall that backs the kitchen. Jot down the time of any fresh scratching in those places.

Reading the trail before anything is set.

The technician starts at the newest sign and works outward. A sketch map records every pellet cluster, oily smudge and chewed carton, and the marks usually converge on one opening. Trapping blindly may bag a few mice while the door stays open.

  • Beneath each sink, then the gaps by the range and refrigerator, finishing at the laundry nook or utility closet.
  • Garage corners: the bottom of the roll-up door, its rubber gasket and the joint between wall and slab.
  • Overhead, check the access hatch, the vents on the roof and each hole a pipe or cable passes through.
  • Ground level: crawl-space vents and skirting, giving the damper side of the house extra time.

Snap traps or stations: how the choice is made.

Moving through the house, the technician selects snap traps or locked stations, lays them along the wall routes mice prefer and keeps them beyond reach of kids and pets. Open floor makes mice nervous and walls do not, so a single trap along a baseboard outperforms several in the middle of a room.

Sealing starts once early catches expose the entries in use. Teeth pull steel wool out on its own, which is why holes get packed mesh topped with flashing or sealant. The sealing page covers materials, and the trapping page compares the device styles.

Habits worth starting today.

For now, leave pellets alone, because sweeping lofts particles. The CDC box further down describes damp wiping for when you do clean, and bait scattered loose indoors is a bad idea.

Storage counts. Put pantry staples and kibble in snap-lid tubs and lift the pet water dish at night. A mouse already inside stays, but the easy buffet that attracts the next one ends.

Slopes and brush beside the house.

Some houses near the Santa Monica Mountains foothills sit beside planted slopes or open brush, and we have no survey of which ones. If yours does, say so on the call. Mice sheltering in ground cover near a wall are close to the foundation gaps we check first, so the technician walks that edge before going inside.

Steps while waiting.

  • Keep the noisiest room closed and let the household know why.
  • Leave the broom in the closet; bristles throw settled grit into the air.
  • Pack away open bait boxes where small hands cannot reach.
  • Note on a pad when each scratch or thump happens.

What public health pages tell you to do.

Read it before lifting a single pellet. It restates what the health agencies recommend, in everyday terms.

  • Pellets and nests turn to powder when dry, so no broom and no shop vac.
  • Attics, garages and sheds hold stale air, so let the breeze flow for half an hour before entering.
  • Skin picks up what gloves block, so wear them before reaching for stained items.
  • Let the liquid sit for around five minutes; the wait does the disinfecting.
  • Knot the bag firmly and put it in an outdoor bin with a lid.
  • Fever or aches in the weeks after contact are worth mentioning to a doctor, along with the rodent exposure.

What Agoura Hills callers ask about mice.

What size of gap lets a mouse in?

Roughly the thickness of a pencil, and slimmer if the edge is soft. Look by drain pipes, behind the dryer, around door frames and where cables leave stucco or siding.

Does warm weather make mice leave?

Not reliably. A cool garage or a shaded crawl space can attract them in summer, and a gap missed in spring often becomes the entry point by fall.

Should I set out hardware-store poison while waiting?

Talk to us before buying any. Poisoned mice frequently die within a wall, and the resulting odor can outdo the original nuisance.

When will the scratching end?

Pace depends on how many animals there are and how many entries they use. A timeline is possible only after the walk-through; one quoted earlier would be a guess.

Is a 3 a.m. call too late?

Not at all. A staffed line takes overnight calls, and the next open visit gets booked.

What to tell us about a mouse problem.

Name the room, the time of night you hear it and what sits under that floor. Any street in Agoura Hills qualifies, and so does each place on the areas page.
