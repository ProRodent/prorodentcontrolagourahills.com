What shows up before the call.

Callers rarely describe a problem in technical terms. They mention what they saw, heard or smelled, and the list below shows the usual starting points.

  • Nesting material in the engine bay of a parked vehicle.
  • A trail of dark pellets along a wall base.
  • Gnawed corners on a pantry door.
  • Daylight visible under the overhead garage door.

Next steps for tonight.

  • Wait to enter the attic or underfloor space until the technician is present; loose insulation floats.
  • A poked nest sends adults farther into the framing.
  • Locate the attic hatch and the underfloor door, then clear the approach.
  • Move boxes off the hatch only if they slide easily.

Five safeguards for droppings and nest scraps.

A little preparation lowers the risk from rodent waste. Order matters: ventilate, wet, wipe, bag, wash.

  • A shop vacuum on a dusty pile is a bad idea; a leaf blower is worse.
  • Plan on half an hour of airing before any wiping begins.
  • Skin picks up what gloves block, so wear them before reaching for stained items.
  • Old bleach mix loses strength, so make a new batch for each session.
  • Waste leaves the house that day instead of sitting on the porch.
  • Wash your forearms along with your palms.
