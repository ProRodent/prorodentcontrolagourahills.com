Trouble in quiet months.

Rodents respond to weather, not the calendar. After a heavy rain, burrows can flood and animals look for higher ground, such as a garage or an attic. In a heat wave, shaded crawl spaces draw them.

Looking before trapping.

Rodents ignore city limits. A fence line or a shared hedge may carry them from the next property to yours, so the walk includes the edges where your lot meets its neighbors.

Early signs worth acting on.

If you recognize any of these, a phone call is a reasonable next step.

  • Tooth marks on a cable jacket in the garage.
  • Shredded paper or insulation bunched in a corner.
  • Greasy rubbed streaks on a baseboard.
  • A dog or cat fixated on one stretch of wall.

A wet-wipe routine for pellets and nests.

Found droppings indoors? This checklist condenses what federal health pages publish, and the links at the end go to the originals.

  • Health agencies tell people not to sweep or vacuum dry rodent waste, so we repeat the warning here.
  • Plan on half an hour of airing before any wiping begins.
  • Choose gloves long enough to cover the wrist, where dust likes to land.
  • Soak the droppings and nests with diluted bleach or a household disinfectant until they shine.
  • Keep pets away from the sealed bag until it is in the bin.
  • Wash your forearms along with your palms.
